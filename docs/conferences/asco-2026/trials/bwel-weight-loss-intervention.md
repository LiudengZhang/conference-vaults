# BWEL (Alliance A011401) — Telephone-based weight loss intervention, stage II/III HER2-negative early breast cancer (BMI ≥27), adjuvant lifestyle

> **Abstract:** 12010 · **Session:** 2026 ASCO Annual Meeting (health outcomes substudy presentation) · **Presenter:** Jennifer A. Ligibel, MD, FASCO (Dana-Farber Cancer Institute, Boston)

## At a glance
- **Sponsor:** National Cancer Institute / NIH (Alliance for Clinical Trials in Oncology). Additional support: Breast Cancer Research Stamp Fund, Susan G. Komen, Breast Cancer Research Foundation, American Cancer Society
- **PI / presenter:** Jennifer A. Ligibel, MD, FASCO — Dana-Farber Cancer Institute
- **NCT ID:** NCT02750826
- **Phase:** 3
- **Design:** Randomized 1:1, phase III. Health education alone vs health education + telephone-based weight loss intervention (up to 42 coach-delivered sessions over 2 years; individualized 500–1,000 kcal/day deficit targeting ~10% weight loss; ramp to 225 min/week moderate activity). The ASCO 2026 readout is the Health Outcomes (HO-1) patient-reported-outcome substudy.
- **N:** 3,180 randomized in the main trial (enrolled 2016–2021 across ~635 practices in US/Canada); HO-1 substudy 542 (intervention n=272, control n=270)
- **Primary endpoint(s):** Main trial — invasive disease-free survival (iDFS, follow-up ongoing). HO-1 substudy — physical function at 6 months
- **Comparator:** Health education alone
- **Indication:** Stage II–III HR+/HER2-negative or triple-negative early breast cancer with BMI ≥27 kg/m², after surgery and chemo/radiation as indicated
- **Line:** Adjuvant (lifestyle intervention)

## Headline result
In the BWEL HO-1 substudy (n=542), the telephone-based weight loss intervention produced clinically meaningful, durable weight loss and improved patient-reported physical function and quality of life. Participants in the intervention arm lost ~5.7% of baseline body weight at 12 months versus controls. At the 6-month primary timepoint, the intervention significantly improved physical function (+1.9 points, P<.001), global physical health (+2.0, P<.001), global mental health (+1.3, P=.03), social roles/activities (+2.3, P<.001), and reduced fatigue (−1.7, P=.01) versus health education alone. Benefits were generally maintained at 2 years after enrollment. The primary invasive disease-free survival endpoint of the parent trial has not yet read out and follow-up is ongoing.

## Mechanism / class
Behavioral lifestyle intervention (not a drug). Mechanistic rationale: obesity is associated with worse breast cancer outcomes; caloric restriction plus increased physical activity reduce adiposity, insulin/IGF signaling, and inflammation. Delivered remotely by trained weight-loss coaches to enable broad, scalable access.

## Discussant takeaway
Not reported in available sources.

## Practice-changing?
**Pending.** The HO-1 substudy establishes that a scalable telephone-based program delivers durable weight loss and meaningful quality-of-life/physical-function gains, strengthening the case for offering structured lifestyle support in survivorship care. Whether weight loss improves cancer outcomes awaits the parent trial's invasive disease-free survival readout; the iDFS result will determine any change to adjuvant treatment standards.
