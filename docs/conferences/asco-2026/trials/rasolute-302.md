# RASolute 302 — Daraxonrasib (RMC-6236) vs chemotherapy, 2L metastatic pancreatic cancer (RAS-mutant and RAS-WT)

> **Abstract:** LBA5 · **Session:** Plenary, Sun May 31, 2026, 3:21 p.m. CT, Hall B1 · **Presenter:** Brian M. Wolpin, MD, MPH (Dana-Farber Cancer Institute) · **Embargo:** lifted 7:00 AM CT May 31, 2026

## At a glance

- **Sponsor:** Revolution Medicines
- **PI / presenter:** Brian M. Wolpin, MD, MPH (Dana-Farber Cancer Institute)
- **NCT ID:** NCT06625320
- **Phase:** 3 (global, multicenter, open-label, randomized, registrational)
- **Design:** Daraxonrasib 300 mg PO once daily vs investigator's choice of standard-of-care cytotoxic chemotherapy; randomized 1:1; ECOG PS 0–1; median age 66
- **N:** 500 (daraxonrasib n=248; chemotherapy n=252) — previously treated metastatic PDAC across a broad range of RAS variants (RAS G12 mutations plus RAS wild-type)
- **Primary endpoint(s):** OS and PFS (both met)
- **Comparator:** Investigator's choice standard-of-care chemotherapy
- **Indication:** Metastatic pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC)
- **Line:** Second-line (previously treated with one prior chemotherapy line)

## Headline result

In the intent-to-treat population, daraxonrasib **nearly doubled median overall survival versus chemotherapy: 13.2 vs 6.7 months (HR 0.40; P<0.0001)** — a ~60% reduction in the risk of death. Median PFS was **7.2 vs 3.6 months** and ORR **31.6% vs 11.2%**. In the **RAS G12–mutant subgroup** (daraxonrasib n=228; chemotherapy n=231), the OS benefit was identical in magnitude (**HR 0.40; 95% CI 0.30–0.54; P = 5.9 × 10⁻¹⁰**), with median PFS **7.3 vs 3.5 months** and ORR **33.2% vs 11.8%**. Grade ≥3 adverse events were less frequent with daraxonrasib (**43.6% vs 57.5%**), as were AE-related discontinuations (**1.2% vs 11.2%**). Median follow-up 8.5 months. The agent was generally well tolerated with no unexpected safety findings, and the results were **simultaneously published in the New England Journal of Medicine**. Widely framed as the headline of the meeting (standing ovation; "transformative" / "unprecedented" in trade coverage). This is the **first positive randomized phase 3 of a RAS-targeted agent in PDAC.**

## Mechanism / class

Daraxonrasib (RMC-6236) is an oral, multi-selective RAS(ON) inhibitor that targets the active (GTP-bound) state of multiple RAS variants (including G12X mutations and wild-type RAS) by forming a tri-complex with cyclophilin A — rather than a single allele (contrast G12C-only inhibitors). Activity in RAS wild-type tumors is consistent with the broad RAS-pathway dependence of PDAC. It is investigational with no prior approvals; RASolute 302 is its first pivotal Phase 3 readout. RASolute 303 is the ongoing first-line registrational trial.

## Discussant takeaway

The pancreatic discussant framed toxicity management and the durability of the OS benefit as the open questions; the magnitude (HR 0.40) was treated as practice-defining for 2L PDAC, a setting with near-static outcomes for a decade. Framed as the first RAS(ON) multi-selective inhibitor to deliver a Phase 3 OS benefit in pancreatic cancer; described by investigators and ASCO press materials as "unprecedented" for this historically chemo-refractory disease.

## Practice-changing?

**Yes** — pending FDA filing. A doubling of median OS (13.2 vs 6.7 mo, HR 0.40) in 2L metastatic PDAC — a setting with minimal effective options — is the strongest signal in this disease in years, with a favorable tolerability profile and simultaneous NEJM publication. It redefines 2L mPDAC and establishes pan-RAS inhibition as a viable class in a tumor where targeted therapy has historically failed. Practice change awaits regulatory action; FDA granted expanded access (May 1, 2026) ahead of approval.
