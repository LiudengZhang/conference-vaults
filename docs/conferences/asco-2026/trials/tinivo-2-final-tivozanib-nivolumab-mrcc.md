# TiNivo-2 (final analysis) — Tivozanib (Fotivda) ± nivolumab (Opdivo), metastatic renal cell carcinoma, second-line after immune checkpoint inhibitor

> **Session:** Genitourinary Cancer — Kidney and Bladder, 2026 ASCO Annual Meeting · **Presenter:** Robert J. Motzer, MD (Memorial Sloan Kettering Cancer Center, New York) · **Embargo:** ASCO 2026 (May 29 – Jun 2, 2026)

## At a glance
- **Sponsor:** AVEO Oncology (an LG Chem company); nivolumab supplied by Bristol Myers Squibb
- **PI / presenter:** Robert J. Motzer, MD (Memorial Sloan Kettering Cancer Center)
- **NCT ID:** NCT04987203
- **Phase:** 3
- **Design:** Randomized, controlled, multicenter, open-label; 1:1 to tivozanib 0.89 mg/day PO (21 days on/7 off) + nivolumab 480 mg IV q4w vs tivozanib monotherapy 1.34 mg/day PO (21 days on/7 off)
- **N:** 343 randomized (combination n=171; monotherapy n=172)
- **Primary endpoint(s):** Progression-free survival (PFS) by blinded independent central review
- **Comparator:** Tivozanib monotherapy (standard 1.34 mg dose)
- **Indication:** Advanced/metastatic renal cell carcinoma after progression on 1–2 prior lines including an immune checkpoint inhibitor (ICI)
- **Line:** Second/third-line (post-ICI)

## Headline result
The final long-term analysis confirmed the negative primary readout: adding nivolumab to low-dose tivozanib did not improve outcomes versus standard-dose tivozanib monotherapy. In the ITT population, median PFS was 5.7 months with tivozanib + nivolumab vs 7.4 months with tivozanib monotherapy (HR 1.10, P=0.49), median OS was 17.7 vs 22.1 months, and ORR was 19.3% vs 19.8%. With extended follow-up approaching 28 months, standard-dose tivozanib monotherapy showed durable activity — median PFS of 7.43 months in the ITT population and 9.23 months in second-line patients, with median OS exceeding 22 months across analyzed populations. The long-term safety profile was consistent with prior reports, with no new safety signals and numerically fewer treatment-emergent adverse events in the combination arm (driven by the lower tivozanib dose). Investigators concluded the data support standard-dose tivozanib monotherapy as an effective option in the second- and third-line post-ICI setting and underscore the limited benefit of ICI retreatment via this combination.

## Mechanism / class
Tivozanib is a potent, selective oral VEGFR-1/2/3 tyrosine kinase inhibitor (VEGFR-TKI), FDA-approved (FOTIVDA) for relapsed/refractory advanced RCC after ≥2 prior systemic therapies on the strength of TIVO-3. Nivolumab is an anti–PD-1 immune checkpoint inhibitor. TiNivo-2 tested whether re-introducing PD-1 blockade alongside a VEGFR-TKI improves outcomes in patients who progressed after a prior ICI — a strategy this trial did not validate.

## Discussant takeaway
The result reinforces that ICI retreatment/continuation after prior checkpoint-inhibitor exposure has not shown benefit in randomized RCC trials (echoing CONTACT-03 and the broader signal against "re-challenge" PD-1/PD-L1 in this setting). A confound is the deliberately reduced tivozanib dose (0.89 mg) in the combination arm versus the full 1.34 mg control dose, which limits clean attribution but does not rescue the combination.

## Practice-changing?
No for the combination — tivozanib + nivolumab is not adopted in the post-ICI setting given the negative primary endpoint and numerically inferior PFS/OS. Indirectly supportive for standard-dose tivozanib monotherapy, whose durable PFS/OS in the control arm reinforces its role as a viable VEGFR-TKI option for second- and third-line post-ICI mRCC.
