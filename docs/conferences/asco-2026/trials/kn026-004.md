# KN026-004 (Neo-Healer) — Anbenitamab + nab-docetaxel ± carboplatin vs THP ± carboplatin, neoadjuvant HER2+ early/locally advanced breast cancer

> **Abstract:** LBA660 · **Session:** Breast Cancer — Local/Regional/Adjuvant (Oral Abstract Session), Sat May 30, 2026, 3:39–3:51 PM CT · **Presenter:** Zhimin Shao, MD, Fudan University Shanghai Cancer Center · **Embargo:** at presentation (May 30, 2026)

## At a glance
- **Sponsor:** Alphamab Oncology (anbenitamab/KN026); HB1801 nab-docetaxel co-developed with JMT-Bio (a CSPC subsidiary). (Research hint named Akeso/Hansoh — corrected to Alphamab/CSPC per sources.)
- **PI / presenter:** Zhimin Shao, MD — Fudan University Shanghai Cancer Center
- **NCT ID:** NCT06747338
- **Phase:** III
- **Design:** Randomized, controlled, open-label, multicenter; 1:1 to anbenitamab + HB1801 (nab-docetaxel) ± carboplatin vs trastuzumab + pertuzumab + docetaxel (THP) ± carboplatin; single-stage continuous 6-cycle neoadjuvant regimen
- **N:** ~520 (randomized 1:1)
- **Primary endpoint(s):** Total pathologic complete response (tpCR, ypT0/is ypN0) by blinded independent review committee (BIRC)
- **Comparator:** THP / TCbHP — trastuzumab + pertuzumab + docetaxel ± carboplatin (standard neoadjuvant dual HER2 blockade)
- **Indication:** HER2-positive early or locally advanced breast cancer
- **Line:** Neoadjuvant (first-line, curative-intent)

## Headline result
In the phase III Neo-Healer trial, neoadjuvant anbenitamab (KN026) plus albumin-bound docetaxel (HB1801) ± carboplatin significantly improved tpCR versus standard THP ± carboplatin, meeting the prespecified primary endpoint with both statistical and clinical significance. Per company and trade-press reports of the LBA660 oral presentation (OncLive/Docwire/CancerNetwork coverage), tpCR by blinded review was **62.4% vs 51.2%** (absolute difference ~11.2 percentage points); investigator-assessed tpCR was **63.9% vs 51.2%**, and breast pCR was **64.6% vs 55.0%** (one-sided *P* = .0099). This is the first head-to-head phase III evidence that a single biparatopic HER2 bispecific can outperform the dual monoclonal-antibody backbone (trastuzumab + pertuzumab) in the neoadjuvant setting, with a reported manageable safety profile.

## Mechanism / class
Anbenitamab (KN026) is an anti-HER2 biparatopic/bispecific antibody built on Alphamab's CRIB (Charge Repulsion Induced Bispecific) Fc-heterodimer platform. It simultaneously binds two non-overlapping HER2 epitopes (ECD subdomains II and IV) — the same domains targeted separately by pertuzumab (II) and trastuzumab (IV) — combining both binding regions in a single molecule for continuous, uninterrupted dual HER2 signal blockade, with enhanced receptor clustering, internalization, and ADCC. HB1801 is an albumin-bound (nab-) docetaxel. The 6-cycle single-stage regimen avoids drug switching/segmented dosing and allows surgery ~6 weeks earlier than the standard schedule. Anbenitamab is investigational and has no approval in breast cancer as of the presentation date; the comparator THP backbone is the established standard for neoadjuvant HER2+ disease.

## Discussant takeaway
The control arm is the pre-DESTINY-Breast11 standard (THP). Commentators noted the result should be read against the evolving neoadjuvant landscape now reshaped by T-DXd → THP (DESTINY-Breast11), which is not the comparator here.

## Practice-changing?
Pending. A positive head-to-head phase III against THP supports anbenitamab as a potential new, simpler single-agent neoadjuvant backbone in place of dual mAb HER2 blockade — if the tpCR gain and event-free/disease-free survival hold up. But the comparator predates T-DXd-based neoadjuvant data, the trial was conducted predominantly in China, and event-free/long-term outcomes are immature. Regulatory and broader practice impact awaits survival follow-up and ex-China data.
