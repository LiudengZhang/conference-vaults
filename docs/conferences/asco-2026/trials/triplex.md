# TRIPLEX — Thoracic radiotherapy added to durvalumab + platinum/etoposide, 1L ES-SCLC

> **Abstract:** LBA8005 · **Session:** Lung Cancer — Late-Breaking Oral Abstract (8000-series, lung track), 2026 ASCO Annual Meeting · **Presenter:** Åslaug Helland, MD, PhD, Oslo University Hospital · **Embargo:** lifts on day of presentation

## At a glance

- **Sponsor:** Norwegian University of Science and Technology (NTNU) — investigator-initiated, Nordic cooperative (not French; the IFCT label sometimes attached to "TRIPLEX" is a mismatch — this is the NTNU/Helland–Grønberg Nordic trial)
- **PI / presenter:** Åslaug Helland (Oslo University Hospital); Bjørn Henning Grønberg (NTNU) trial group
- **NCT ID:** NCT05223647
- **Phase:** 3
- **Design:** Randomized open-label. Experimental arm = carboplatin/etoposide + durvalumab **plus thoracic radiotherapy 30 Gy/10 fractions delivered between cycles 2 and 3**, then durvalumab maintenance. Control arm = carboplatin/etoposide + durvalumab → durvalumab maintenance (CASPIAN-style backbone).
- **N:** 239 (actual enrollment)
- **Primary endpoint(s):** Overall survival (change in 1-year OS; Cox PH, evaluated ~14 months post-enrollment)
- **Comparator:** chemo-immunotherapy alone (active control)
- **Indication:** extensive-stage small-cell lung cancer (ES-SCLC)
- **Line:** first-line
- **Geography:** Norway, Sweden, Iceland, Estonia, Netherlands

## Headline result

The trial was **terminated early on the advice of its Data Safety Committee due to excess toxicity in the experimental (added thoracic radiotherapy) arm** (ClinicalTrials.gov, status verified Jan 2026). The LBA8005 presentation reports outcomes from the 239 randomized patients. **Detailed efficacy results — OS hazard ratio, p-value, and median OS by arm — were under embargo and not publicly available at the time of writing; numbers are not reproduced here to avoid fabrication.** The load-bearing, sourced finding to date is the safety-driven early stop: adding concurrent/interleaved thoracic radiotherapy (30 Gy/10 fx between cycles 2–3) to first-line carboplatin/etoposide + durvalumab was not deliverable at an acceptable toxicity threshold in this design.

## Mechanism / class

Durvalumab is an anti–PD-L1 monoclonal antibody; the chemo-IO backbone (platinum/etoposide + durvalumab) is the CASPIAN-derived 1L ES-SCLC standard. The trial's hypothesis was that **thoracic radiotherapy** added to chemo-IO would improve OS via local control and radiation-induced immunogenic priming of checkpoint blockade — the same rationale validated by ADRIATIC (consolidation durvalumab after chemoRT) in *limited*-stage SCLC, extended here to extensive-stage with radiotherapy interleaved early during induction.

## Practice-changing?

**No (negative / cautionary).** An early termination for excess toxicity argues against routinely interleaving 30 Gy/10 fx thoracic radiotherapy between induction cycles of chemo-IO in ES-SCLC. The result reframes the consolidative-TRT question (RTOG/CREST-era and ongoing trials) and the SAKK 15/19 sequencing approach (TRT *after* chemo-IO) as the safer scheduling paradigm. Pending the embargoed OS data, the practical takeaway is that *timing/schedule* of TRT in the IO era matters and concurrent-with-induction is poorly tolerated.
