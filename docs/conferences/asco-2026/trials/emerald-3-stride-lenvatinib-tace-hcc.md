# EMERALD-3 — Durvalumab + tremelimumab (STRIDE) ± lenvatinib + TACE, unresectable embolization-eligible HCC, locoregional/1L

> **Abstract:** LBA4000 · **Session:** Gastrointestinal Cancer (Hepatobiliary), 2026 ASCO Annual Meeting · **Presenter:** Ghassan K. Abou-Alfa, MD (Memorial Sloan Kettering Cancer Center) · **Embargo:** per ASCO LBA release

## At a glance
- **Sponsor:** AstraZeneca
- **PI / presenter:** Ghassan K. Abou-Alfa, MD — Memorial Sloan Kettering Cancer Center (principal investigator / presenter)
- **NCT ID:** NCT05301842
- **Phase:** 3
- **Design:** Randomized, open-label, multicenter (171 centers, 22 countries); three arms — STRIDE + lenvatinib + TACE vs STRIDE + TACE vs TACE alone
- **N:** 760 (STRIDE + lenvatinib + TACE n=293; STRIDE + TACE n=175; TACE alone n=292)
- **Primary endpoint(s):** Progression-free survival (PFS) for STRIDE + lenvatinib + TACE vs TACE alone
- **Comparator:** TACE alone
- **Indication:** Unresectable, embolization-eligible hepatocellular carcinoma (eeHCC)
- **Line:** Locoregional / first systemic-plus-locoregional therapy

## Headline result
In the planned analysis, STRIDE (single tremelimumab regular-interval durvalumab) plus lenvatinib plus TACE significantly improved median PFS to **13.0 vs 9.8 months** vs TACE alone (**HR 0.70**), a 30% reduction in the risk of progression or death. The STRIDE + TACE arm (without lenvatinib) also improved PFS, **12.9 vs 8.1 months** (HR 0.71). At an interim OS analysis (~40% maturity), STRIDE + lenvatinib + TACE showed an early favorable trend (median OS 39.5 vs 34.7 mo; HR 0.84; 24-mo OS 66.9% vs 61.5%), and STRIDE + TACE vs TACE showed median OS not reached vs 32.9 mo (HR 0.70; 24-mo OS 68.0% vs 57.8%); OS remained immature and follow-up is ongoing. P-values and confidence intervals were not detailed in available trade-press coverage at time of writing.

## Mechanism / class
STRIDE = a single priming dose of tremelimumab (anti–CTLA-4) added to regular-interval durvalumab (anti–PD-L1), the regimen approved in unresectable HCC on the HIMALAYA trial. Lenvatinib is an oral multikinase/VEGFR-directed TKI. TACE is locoregional transarterial chemoembolization. EMERALD-3 tests whether layering dual checkpoint blockade ± antiangiogenic TKI onto TACE deepens locoregional control in embolization-eligible disease.

## Discussant takeaway
Not captured in available coverage at time of writing; PI Abou-Alfa framed the result as establishing STRIDE as an immunotherapy backbone alongside lenvatinib and TACE for embolization-eligible HCC.

## Practice-changing?
Pending. EMERALD-3 is the second positive immunotherapy-plus-TACE phase 3 in embolization-eligible HCC (after EMERALD-1, durvalumab + bevacizumab + TACE). The PFS gain is statistically significant and clinically meaningful, but OS is immature and a clear OS benefit will likely be required for practice change and regulatory filing in this curative-intent-adjacent setting. The STRIDE + TACE (no lenvatinib) arm performing comparably on PFS raises questions about lenvatinib's marginal contribution.
