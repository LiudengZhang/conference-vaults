# OptiTROP-Lung05 — Sac-TMT (TROP2 ADC) + pembrolizumab vs pembrolizumab, first-line PD-L1+ advanced NSCLC

> **Abstract:** 8506 · **Session:** Lung Cancer — Metastatic Non-Small Cell (Oral), Fri May 29, 2026 · **Presenter:** Caicun Zhou, Shanghai East Hospital, Tongji University · **Embargo:** May 29, 2026

## At a glance
- **Sponsor:** Sichuan Kelun-Biotech Biopharmaceutical Co. (developer); Merck/MSD (collaborator, exclusive rights outside Greater China)
- **PI / presenter:** Caicun Zhou (Shanghai East Hospital, Tongji University)
- **NCT ID:** *not confirmed in trade press / ASCO abstract surfaced — omitted to avoid error*
- **Phase:** 3
- **Design:** Randomized, open-label, multicenter; 1:1 randomization
- **N:** 413
- **Primary endpoint(s):** PFS by blinded independent central review (BICR)
- **Comparator:** Pembrolizumab monotherapy
- **Indication:** Locally advanced or metastatic NSCLC, PD-L1 TPS ≥1%, without EGFR/ALK alterations
- **Line:** First-line (treatment-naïve)

## Headline result
At interim analysis (data cutoff Sep 29, 2025; median follow-up 10.5 mo), sac-TMT + pembrolizumab significantly prolonged PFS vs pembrolizumab alone: median PFS by BICR **not reached vs 5.7 months** (HR 0.35; 95% CI 0.26–0.47; p<0.0001) — a 65% reduction in risk of progression or death; 12-month PFS 62.4% vs 29.0%. ORR was 70.2% vs 42.0% and deep response rate 49.0% vs 25.9%; 12-month DoR 77.7% vs 59.4%. Overall survival, immature at interim, trended in favor of the combination: median NR vs 14.5 months (HR 0.55; 95% CI 0.36–0.85), a ~45% reduction in risk of death; 12-month OS 80.4% vs 68.9%. Grade ≥3 treatment-emergent AEs were higher with the combination (primarily hematologic); no sac-TMT-related deaths and no new safety signals. Simultaneously published in *The Lancet*.

## Mechanism / class
Sacituzumab tirumotecan (sac-TMT, SKB264/MK-2870) is a TROP2-directed antibody-drug conjugate carrying a belotecan-derivative topoisomerase-I inhibitor payload via a hydrolyzable linker. Pembrolizumab is an anti–PD-1 checkpoint inhibitor. This is the first randomized phase 3 trial to show a PFS benefit for an ADC + checkpoint inhibitor combination over checkpoint inhibitor alone in first-line PD-L1–positive advanced NSCLC.

## Practice-changing?
Pending. The PFS benefit is large and consistent with maturing OS, ORR, and depth-of-response signals, positioning the combination as a potential new 1L standard for PD-L1–positive NSCLC without driver alterations. Confirmation awaits mature OS, regulatory review, and the China-heavy trial population's generalizability.
