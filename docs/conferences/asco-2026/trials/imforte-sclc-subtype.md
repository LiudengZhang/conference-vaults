# IMforte (subtype analysis) — Lurbinectedin + atezolizumab, 1L maintenance, extensive-stage SCLC

> **Abstract:** 8014 · **Session:** Rapid Oral Abstract Session — Lung Cancer-Non-Small Cell Local-Regional/Small Cell/Other Thoracic Cancers, Sun May 31 2026, 4:30–6:00 PM CT · **Presenter:** Luis Paz-Ares, MD, PhD (Hospital Universitario 12 de Octubre, Madrid) · **Embargo:** at presentation

## At a glance
- **Sponsor:** F. Hoffmann-La Roche / Genentech; co-development with Jazz Pharmaceuticals (Zepzelca)
- **PI / presenter:** Luis Paz-Ares (co-authors incl. Borghaei, Reck, Peters, Herbst)
- **NCT ID:** NCT05091567
- **Phase:** 3 (this report = exploratory transcriptomic/biomarker subanalysis)
- **Design:** Randomized, open-label maintenance trial; this abstract is a transcriptomic analysis of molecular subsets (SCLC-A / SCLC-N / SCLC-P / SCLC-I, inflamed signatures) correlated with maintenance outcomes
- **N:** 483 randomized into maintenance (lurbi+atezo n=242; atezo n=241); biomarker-evaluable subset per abstract
- **Primary endpoint(s):** Parent trial — IRF-PFS and OS. Subanalysis — subtype-specific PFS/OS treatment effect
- **Comparator:** Atezolizumab maintenance alone
- **Indication:** Extensive-stage small-cell lung cancer (ES-SCLC), post-induction (carboplatin + etoposide + atezolizumab ×4)
- **Line:** First-line maintenance

## Headline result
The parent phase 3 IMforte trial established that lurbinectedin + atezolizumab maintenance significantly improved both PFS (median 5.4 vs 2.1 months; HR 0.54, p<0.0001 by IRF) and OS (median 13.2 vs 10.6 months; HR 0.73, p≈0.017) versus atezolizumab alone, with confirmed ORR 19.4% vs 10.4% — the basis for FDA approval (Oct 2025) and CHMP positive opinion. Abstract 8014 reports the transcriptomic subanalysis examining whether the SCLC-A/N/P/I molecular subtypes drive the maintenance benefit. *Subtype-specific hazard ratios and signature correlations were not available in pre-presentation trade-press coverage as of this writing; the analysis is framed as exploratory/hypothesis-generating and the abstract poses whether a subtype signal exists rather than asserting a predictive biomarker.*

## Mechanism / class
Lurbinectedin is a selective inhibitor of oncogenic transcription (binds the DNA minor groove, degrades elongating RNA Pol II, disrupts tumor-associated macrophage transcription). Atezolizumab is an anti–PD-L1 monoclonal antibody. The combination exploits lurbinectedin's transcription-targeting plus immune modulation as low-burden maintenance after platinum/etoposide/atezolizumab induction. SCLC molecular subtypes are defined by lineage transcription factors: SCLC-A (ASCL1), SCLC-N (NEUROD1), SCLC-P (POU2F3), and SCLC-I (inflamed/immune-enriched, "triple-negative"), the last historically most immunotherapy-responsive.

## Practice-changing?
Pending. The maintenance regimen itself is already standard of care (approved). This subtype analysis is unlikely to change practice on its own unless a strong predictive signal emerges; more plausibly it informs future biomarker-stratified trial design and the ongoing question of whether SCLC-I derives disproportionate immunotherapy benefit. Treat as biology/hypothesis-generating until subtype HRs are reported.
