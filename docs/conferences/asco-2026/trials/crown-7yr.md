# CROWN — Lorlatinib vs crizotinib, 1L advanced ALK+ NSCLC (7-year update)

> **Abstract:** 8502 · **Session:** Lung Cancer — Non-Small Cell Metastatic, Oral Abstract Session, Fri May 29, 2026 · **Presenter:** Tony S. K. Mok, MD (Chinese University of Hong Kong) · **Embargo:** May 29, 2026

## At a glance

- **Sponsor:** Pfizer Inc.
- **PI / presenter:** Tony S. K. Mok, MD — Chinese University of Hong Kong, Dept. of Clinical Oncology
- **NCT ID:** NCT03052608
- **Phase:** 3
- **Design:** Randomized, open-label, 1:1 lorlatinib vs crizotinib; previously untreated advanced/metastatic ALK+ NSCLC. This is the 7-year (longest follow-up) update, an unplanned post-hoc analysis prompted by median PFS still being unreached at the 3- and 5-year marks; simultaneous publication in *Annals of Oncology*.
- **N:** 296 (lorlatinib n=149; crizotinib n=147)
- **Primary endpoint(s):** Progression-free survival (blinded independent central review at primary analysis; investigator-assessed PFS reported in long-term updates)
- **Comparator:** Crizotinib (XALKORI)
- **Indication:** Advanced / metastatic ALK-positive NSCLC
- **Line:** First-line (1L)

## Headline result

At a median follow-up of **83.0 months** (lorlatinib) vs **77.2 months** (crizotinib), investigator-assessed median PFS remained **not reached** with lorlatinib vs **9.1 months** with crizotinib, HR **0.19 (95% CI 0.13–0.26)** — an 81% reduction in the risk of progression or death. The probability of being alive and progression-free at 7 years was **55% (95% CI 46–63)** with lorlatinib vs **3% (95% CI 1–8)** with crizotinib — the longest PFS reported to date in advanced NSCLC. Intracranial benefit was maintained: time to intracranial progression not reached vs **16.4 months (12.7–21.9)**, HR **0.06 (95% CI 0.03–0.12)**, with no new intracranial progression events after 30 months. Longer follow-up showed very few additional PFS events; 44% of patients remained on lorlatinib at data cutoff, and patients event-free at 24 months had a 79% probability of 7-year PFS. Grade 3/4 AEs were higher with lorlatinib (77% vs 57%) but permanent discontinuation was low (5% vs 6%) with no new discontinuations after 26 months; dose reductions (34%) did not compromise efficacy. Overall survival data remain immature and are to be reported separately.

## Mechanism / class

Lorlatinib (LORBRENA / Lorviqua) is a third-generation, brain-penetrant ALK/ROS1 tyrosine kinase inhibitor designed to cross the blood–brain barrier and to retain activity against a broad spectrum of ALK resistance mutations. FDA-approved for 1L ALK+ metastatic NSCLC (2021) on the basis of the CROWN primary analysis. Crizotinib is a first-generation ALK/ROS1/MET TKI.

## Discussant takeaway

With median PFS still unreached at 7 years and 55% of patients progression-free, the discussion framed CROWN as setting a durability benchmark for oncogene-addicted NSCLC: an unprecedented PFS plateau, near-total CNS protection (intracranial HR 0.06, no new events after 30 months), and a long-term safety profile manageable by dose reduction without loss of efficacy. The principal caveat remains immature overall survival, to be reported separately.

## Practice-changing?

Confirmatory rather than newly practice-changing. Lorlatinib is already an established first-line standard for ALK+ NSCLC; the 7-year update reinforces rather than changes practice. It entrenches lorlatinib as the preferred 1L option — an unprecedented plateau in PFS, durable CNS protection, and a manageable long-term safety profile — reinforcing existing NCCN/ESMO preferences without altering the standard of care.
